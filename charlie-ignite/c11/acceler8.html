The tool: belly breathing

  1. One hand on your belly. Breathe in through the nose so the hand rises, not the chest.
  2. Make every breath out at least as long as the breath in.
  3. On the out-breath, hum. The vibration lets the tension go.
  4. Five minutes is enough. Five breaths is enough before a pitch.

Stressed breathing is fast, shallow and in the chest. Noticing it is the first self-awareness tool of the day.

Try this week
Five belly breaths before every call that matters. Notice what your hands and feet are doing.

Self awareness

Selica's session: emotions as information, and four tools for reading your own
Self-awareness is having a good knowledge and understanding of yourself and being aware of your feelings as they change.Selica
As an entrepreneur, you can align your strengths and your weaknesses with your business model, your team, and your role in order to produce results.Why it matters
Our emotions are a snapshot in time.
The more that you practice being in tune with how you feel and actually finding the proper language for labeling how you feel will also fine tune your filter.

Four tools to know yourself

  • The Feelings Wheel. "Mad" is a label. "Frustrated because I was overlooked" is information you can act on.
  • Keep a journal. What happened, how you felt, what you were thinking at the time. Look for patterns. Prompts from the slides: What makes you feel powerful? What makes you feel calm? What makes you feel in control? How do you encourage yourself when you try something new?
  • Notice your breath. It changes before you know you are stressed.
  • Ask for feedback: the Johari Window. Four quadrants. What you and others both know (open), what only you know (hidden), what others see and you do not (blind), and what nobody knows yet. Feedback shrinks the blind spot.

Emotional intelligence has five parts: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill. Self-awareness is the one the other four stand on.

Entrepreneurial leadership

Lollipop moments, leader versus manager, six styles, and your Leadership Canvas
How many of you guys have a lollipop moment? A moment where someone said something or did something that you feel fundamentally made your life better?After the lollipop moment video
We let people who have made our lives better walk around without knowing it.

Leader or manager?

A leader sets the vision and inspires people to follow it. A manager executes the vision and organises the resources. Managing is working in your business; leading is working on it. Most founders slide into managing without noticing.

What makes it entrepreneurial

  • Optimising risk, not avoiding it.
  • Taking personal responsibility. The buck stops with you.
  • Innovating with no budget to hire the people you need.
  • Managing a dynamic environment where the future is uncertain.

Goleman's six styles

Nobody has one style. The skill is knowing your fall-back and choosing another when the moment needs it. Where do you sit on the scale between love and fear?

Personal effectiveness

The thought loop, five twisted thinking habits, and how to untwist each one
Congratulations, you made an ugly baby.On perfectionism, and why you should launch it anyway
The tool against perfectionism is: if it fails, is it detrimental?
Often times what is the worst case scenario is not as bad as the emotional runoff that we do while catastrophizing something.
The tool to combat jumping to conclusions is going back to the data.

The loop

Thoughts ("I always mess this up") lead to feelings (anxious, self-conscious), which show up in the body (tense, palpitations, drained), which change your behaviour (withdraw, stop doing what you enjoy). The loop runs both ways. Change the thought and the rest follows.

"Thoughts become perception, perception becomes reality. Alter your thoughts, alter your reality." William James, from the closing slide.

Try this week

Coaching each other

Four fundamentals, the GROW model, and ten minutes each way

The four fundamentals

  • Build trust and rapport before you build anything else.
  • Listen actively, with your body as well as your ears.
  • Give supportive feedback.
  • Ask the right questions. Not give the answer.

Peer coaching with GROW

  1. Goal. What do you want to achieve? What does good look like?
  2. Reality. Where are you now? What have you tried? Who is involved?
  3. Options. What could you do? Who could help? Brainstorm before you judge.
  4. Way forward. What will you do, by when, and how will you know?

Thinking errors, especially the inner critic, are hard to untwist alone. A coach who only asks questions can see the options you cannot. Then: name it, own it, believe, growth mindset.

Try this week
Ten minutes each way with a mastermind partner. Coach: questions only. Coachee: leave with one action and a date.
